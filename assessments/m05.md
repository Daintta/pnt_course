# Module 05 assessment: Alternative PNT Technologies

## match
Classify each source by the type of information it provides.
- eLoran = Absolute / externally referenced
- Wheel odometry = Relative / self-contained
- GNSS + INS integration = Hybrid of absolute and relative
> Absolute sources reference something external; relative sources propagate from a known state; hybrids let one constrain the weaknesses of another.

## match
Match each inertial sensor to what it measures.
- Accelerometer = Specific force along an axis
- Gyroscope = Angular rate or rotation
- Magnetometer = Local magnetic field, giving heading-related information
> An IMU typically contains accelerometers and gyroscopes. A magnetometer is sometimes integrated but is not intrinsically an inertial sensor.

## order
Put the INS mechanisation steps in a sensible order.
1. Start from a known initial position, velocity and attitude
2. Integrate angular-rate measurements to update orientation
3. Transform measured acceleration into the navigation frame and account for gravity
4. Integrate acceleration to estimate velocity
5. Integrate velocity to estimate position
> Known initial state + measured rotation + measured acceleration → propagated position, velocity and attitude.

## single
Why is accelerometer bias particularly damaging in inertial navigation?
- Because it only affects heading.
* Because it is integrated to velocity and then again to position, so a small persistent error grows into a substantial position error.
- Because accelerometers cannot be calibrated.
- Because it causes GNSS signals to be lost.
> Double integration makes a small bias grow rapidly. Drift is unbounded without an external constraint.

## single
A proposal states "the platform has an IMU, so it can navigate without GNSS". What is the most useful response?
- Accept it: all IMUs provide equivalent navigation performance.
* Ask about sensor grade, bias stability, alignment, calibration, environmental limits and demonstrated drift over the required outage duration.
- Reject it: IMUs cannot be used for navigation.
- Ask only for the IMU's update rate.
> Inertial sensors span a huge performance range. "Has an IMU" says almost nothing about navigation performance.

## truefalse
Adding more sensors to a fusion filter always improves the navigation solution.
= false
> Poor calibration, incorrect uncertainty models, correlated errors or a faulty sensor can degrade a fused estimate. Fusion needs monitoring and fault handling.

## single
What is clock holdover?
- The time taken for a clock to warm up after power-on.
* The ability of a timing system to maintain an acceptable time estimate after its external reference is lost, with uncertainty growing over time.
- A method of distributing time over a network.
- A permanent replacement for GNSS timing.
> Holdover is the timing equivalent of inertial drift: it bridges outages, but uncertainty grows.

## match
Match each timing term to its meaning.
- Synchronisation = Agreement in time or phase between clocks
- Syntonisation = Agreement in frequency, so clocks advance at the same rate
- Allan deviation = Statistical measure of frequency stability over different averaging times
- Resolution = Smallest increment that can be represented or measured
> A system can be well syntonised yet retain a time offset. High resolution does not by itself imply high accuracy.

## multi
Which statements about emerging quantum and AI/ML-assisted PNT reflect the module's guidance?
* Quantum PNT is a family of developing technologies, not a finished replacement for GPS.
* An AI-enabled estimator may behave unpredictably outside its training and validation conditions.
* Technology maturity should be treated as an assurance claim to be evidenced.

## single
A vehicle enters a long tunnel and loses GNSS. Which statement best describes a well-engineered integrated system?
- The navigation solution fails immediately at the tunnel portal.
- The solution stays as accurate as it was with GNSS for the whole tunnel.
* The INS, wheel speed and map constraints propagate the solution while its uncertainty grows, and GNSS re-constrains it at the exit.
- The system should stop reporting position until GNSS returns.
> Dead reckoning can bridge the outage, but uncertainty grows. A good system knows and reports the uncertainty of its propagated solution.
