# Module 03 assessment: GNSS Signals, Errors and Performance

## single
A receiver has seven satellites and produces a position solution. What does the existence of that solution tell you about its quality?
- It proves the solution is accurate to within the receiver's specification.
* Very little on its own: quality depends on the measurement error budget and the satellite geometry.
- It proves the geometry is good, because at least four satellites were used.
- It proves integrity, because the mathematics converged.
> A receiver can solve successfully yet still contain significant error. Measurement errors and geometry together determine quality.

Which of these contribute to the GNSS pseudorange error budget?
* Satellite orbit error
* Ionospheric delay
* Multipath and NLOS reception
* Receiver and instrumental effects
- The user's intended destination
> Measured pseudorange = geometric range + clock, propagation and equipment effects + multipath + noise.

## single
Why is dual-frequency measurement particularly effective against ionospheric delay?
- Because the ionosphere does not affect higher frequencies at all.
* Because ionospheric delay is dispersive, so comparing frequencies reveals and removes much of the first-order effect.
- Because dual-frequency receivers use atomic clocks.
- Because the second frequency is always transmitted from a different constellation.
> The ionosphere affects different frequencies differently. That difference provides information that single-frequency measurements cannot observe in the same way.

## truefalse
Dual-frequency measurement removes tropospheric delay in the same way it removes first-order ionospheric delay.
= false
> The troposphere is broadly non-dispersive at GNSS frequencies. Its delay is normally modelled or estimated, not cancelled by frequency comparison.

## match
Match each error source to its description.
- Ionospheric delay = Charged upper atmosphere; dispersive, so multi-frequency combinations can mitigate it
- Tropospheric delay = Neutral lower atmosphere; broadly non-dispersive, so it is modelled or estimated
- Multipath = Reflected copies arriving alongside the direct signal and distorting timing
- Receiver noise = Thermal noise and finite signal strength within the equipment
> Ionosphere: charged and dispersive. Troposphere: neutral and modelled. Multipath and receiver effects arise near and within the user equipment.

## single
Why are low-elevation satellites generally more affected by atmospheric delay?
- Because they transmit at lower power.
* Because their signals travel through a longer effective path through the atmosphere.
- Because they belong to older constellations.
- Because the almanac is less accurate for them.
> A low-elevation signal crosses more atmosphere, increasing and complicating both ionospheric and tropospheric effects.

## single
In an urban street, the direct signal from a satellite is blocked and the receiver tracks only a reflection from a building. What is this called and what is its effect?
- Multipath; it shortens the measured range.
* Non-line-of-sight (NLOS) reception; the apparent path is longer than the direct range, potentially biasing the measurement significantly.
- Ionospheric scintillation; it causes loss of lock.
- Masking; it has no effect on the measurement.
> In NLOS the receiver tracks only an indirect signal, so the measured path is longer than the true geometric range.

## single
A receiver designer allows reflected signals to be used in difficult environments. What trade-off does this represent?
* It can improve availability by keeping more measurements, while degrading accuracy.
- It improves both availability and accuracy with no downside.
- It reduces availability but improves integrity.
- It has no effect because reflected signals are ignored by the solution.
> Accepting lower-quality measurements can keep a solution available, but at the cost of accuracy. The right trade-off depends on the application.

## match
Match each measurement type to its characteristic.
- Code pseudorange = Relatively noisy but unambiguous
- Carrier phase = Highly precise but contains an unknown integer number of wavelengths
> Code measurements are unambiguous but noisy. Carrier phase is precise but initially ambiguous, which is central to RTK and PPP.

## single
Which statement about Dilution of Precision (DOP) is correct?
- DOP is the ranging error caused by the atmosphere.
* DOP is a dimensionless geometry factor that amplifies existing measurement uncertainty into solution uncertainty.
- A low DOP guarantees an accurate position even with corrupted measurements.
- DOP only applies to timing solutions.
> DOP does not create ranging error. It describes how geometry magnifies measurement uncertainty. A low DOP cannot rescue bad measurements.

## match
Match each DOP term to what it describes.
- GDOP = Position and time together
- PDOP = Three-dimensional position
- HDOP = Horizontal position
- VDOP = Vertical position
- TDOP = Time
> Each DOP term describes dilution in a different part of the navigation solution.

## single
Why is GNSS vertical accuracy commonly worse than horizontal accuracy?
- Satellites transmit weaker signals in the vertical direction.
* Satellites are observed above the user rather than all around, giving weaker vertical geometry, so VDOP is often larger than HDOP.
- Height is not part of the navigation solution.
- Vertical errors are caused only by the troposphere.
> Satellites are not distributed below the user, so vertical geometry is inherently less favourable for most users.

## truefalse
Two receivers using the same constellation at the same location will always produce identical performance.
= false
> Antenna design, receiver architecture, algorithms, installation and the surrounding environment all affect performance.

## multi
A vehicle drives from open sky into an urban canyon. Which effects can occur at the same time?
* Measurement quality degrades through multipath and NLOS.
* The number of usable measurements falls.
* DOP worsens because the visible satellites are concentrated in a strip of sky.
- The ionospheric delay disappears because buildings block it.
> All three can happen together. The receiver may still output a fix, but its performance may have changed significantly.

## single
A supplier states "our receiver is accurate to one metre". Which follow-up question is most important for turning this into a verifiable assurance claim?
- What colour is the receiver housing?
- How many satellites are in each constellation?
* Is it horizontal, vertical or 3D, at what statistical confidence, under what conditions, and against what reference truth?
- Does the receiver support a touchscreen?
> A performance number without dimension, statistics, conditions and reference truth is not yet a claim you can meaningfully verify.
